D1 — Reconstructing Complete Dialogue Threads with Graph Search
DATA ARCHITECTURE
The Problem
Raw tweets in twcs.csv are broken into fragmented, out-of-order pieces. Trying to classify an isolated opening tweet without seeing what happened next leads to massive confusion.
The Decision
Built a breadth-first search (BFS) graph algorithm to stitch together 12,443 complete, chronological passenger-agent conversation threads.
The Proof
Evaluating intents on reconstructed threads improved Macro-F1 by +6.0%, because downstream agent replies clarify ambiguous opening tweets.
Ruled Out
Classifying single tweets in isolation, which leaves out crucial follow-up context.
D2 — 100% Hand-Labelled Golden Test Benchmark (250 Rows)
BENCHMARK INTEGRITY
The Problem
Many projects use LLMs to label their own test set ("proxy gold"). This causes self-grading bias and artificial score inflation.
The Decision
Created and manually hand-annotated a dedicated 250-row human golden set (10 balanced classes × 25 rows) with strict candidate immutability.
The Proof
Achieved 100% human review across all 250 rows with zero missing values and zero synthetic label contamination.
Ruled Out
Claiming "high accuracy" using synthetic or model-bootstrapped test labels.
D3 — Stratified 3-Tier Sampling over Naive Random Selection
SAMPLING METHODOLOGY
The Problem
Random uniform sampling over-samples easy high-frequency complaints and ignores rare, dangerous airline crises.
The Decision
Divided the golden set into three distinct tiers: (1) 100 Representative samples, (2) 100 Low-Confidence Boundary samples, and (3) 50 Hard Adversarial samples (sarcasm, hostile rants, multi-issue queries).
The Proof
Exposed critical system weaknesses (like sarcasm and gate disputes) during development rather than in production.
Ruled Out
Blind uniform sampling that hides long-tail edge-case failures.
D4 — 100% Rejection of "General Dissatisfaction" Catch-All Bucket
TAXONOMY DESIGN
The Problem
Unsupervised clustering suggested dumping half of all angry customer tweets into a lazy "General Dissatisfaction" supercluster.
The Decision
100% rejected this bucket during human annotation, forcing every complaint into actionable airline categories: Flight Delays, Baggage, Seats, or Disruption.
The Proof
0 out of 250 rows accepted the vague candidate label; produced a clean, operationally distinct 10-class airline taxonomy.
Ruled Out
Lazy catch-all sentiment categories that human support agents cannot act on.
D5 — Real-Time Cross-Field Validation Rules in Annotation App
DATA QUALITY
The Problem
When humans annotate hundreds of rows, fatigue leads to accidental contradictions (e.g. clicking ACCEPT while typing a replacement label).
The Decision
Programmed real-time schema validation rules in src/customer_support/labeling/validation.py directly inside the Streamlit labeling tool.
The Proof
Prevented human input contradictions before saving, guaranteeing zero schema errors in the final golden set.
Ruled Out
Unchecked free-text spreadsheet labeling that corrupts golden evaluation data.
D6 — Throwing Away 600 Synthetic LLM-Generated Rows
DATA PRUNING
The Problem
We initially tried generating 600 LLM-labeled synthetic rows to artificially expand our small training dataset.
The Decision
Completely discarded all 600 synthetic rows after testing, keeping training strictly restricted to verified human data.
The Proof
The 600 synthetic rows gave exactly +0.0 Macro-F1 improvement while adding label noise to fine boundary classes.
Ruled Out
Blindly scaling synthetic data quantity over real human annotation quality.
D7 — Full Conversation Representation for Contextual Feature Learning
FEATURE ENGINEERING
The Problem
Live production tweets only contain the customer opener, but historical data contains the full agent exchange.
The Decision
Trained the embedding body on full multi-turn conversation text while configuring the inference pipeline to handle customer-only openers.
The Proof
Anchored transformer attention on verified historical resolution cues, boosting test accuracy by +4.0%.
Ruled Out
Throwing away historical agent resolution signals during offline model training.
D8 — Hardware Acceleration on Local NVIDIA RTX 4050 Laptop GPU
COMPUTE INFRASTRUCTURE
The Problem
Running deep transformer models on CPU was slow, making contrastive parameter tuning painfully sluggish.
The Decision
Configured PyTorch CUDA 12.4 to harness the machine's NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4050 Laptop GPU (6GB VRAM) for batched tensor math.
The Proof
Encoding speed exceeded 400 sentences/sec; entire end-to-end evaluation harness runs in under 40 seconds.
Ruled Out
Restricting experiments to weak CPU-only models due to compute bottlenecks.
D9 — Rejecting Massive Cross-Entropy Transformer Fine-Tuning
MODEL SELECTION
The Problem
Standard deep transformers (like RoBERTa-base with 125M parameters) require tens of thousands of samples to avoid severe overfitting.
The Decision
Ruled out standard cross-entropy fine-tuning of massive parameter heads on our 200-sample training split.
The Proof
RoBERTa cross-entropy collapsed into the majority class (16.0% accuracy), completely failing on few-shot data.
Ruled Out
Brute-force deep neural fine-tuning without few-shot metric learning constraints.
D10 — Contrastive Metric Learning via SetFit on Airline Tweets
FEW-SHOT LEARNING
The Problem
Pre-trained out-of-the-box sentence embeddings scored only 56.0% accuracy because general text models do not understand airline slang.
The Decision
Fine-tuned the MiniLM transformer backbone using SetFit contrastive cosine loss (pulling similar airline queries together, pushing others away).
The Proof
Lifted baseline accuracy from 56.0% to 66.0% (+10.0% gain) without requiring thousands of labeled samples.
Ruled Out
Relying on frozen, off-the-shelf sentence embeddings without airline domain adaptation.
D11 — Dual-Backbone Ensemble: Domain Contrastive + Foundation Understanding
ENSEMBLE SOTA (74%)
The Problem
Lightweight MiniLM (384d) is fast but struggles with complex, sarcastic, or multi-sentence customer rants.
The Decision
Created a dual-backbone ensemble combining domain-adapted SetFit-MiniLM with high-capacity BGE-Large (1024d) foundation embeddings.
The Proof
Test accuracy leapt to 74.0% (+18.0% over baseline), with Baggage issues and Flight delays reaching 100% recall.
Ruled Out
Betting on a single small model architecture for complex conversational Twitter text.
D12 — Prior Odds Margin Calibration for Low-Frequency Classes
WINNING SOTA (76%)
The Problem
Training data had a 4:1 imbalance between Positive experience and Social acknowledgement, causing casual "Hi" tweets to be misclassified as praise.
The Decision
Applied prior odds margin calibration to the blended ensemble margins to give rare classes a fair mathematical chance.
The Proof
Doubled recall on Social acknowledgement from 25% to 50%, producing our winning headline score of 76.0% accuracy (0.7179 Macro-F1).
Ruled Out
Raw uncalibrated argmax predictions that favor dominant majority classes.
D13 — Strict Liability Safety Gating with Explicit Reason Codes
ROUTING & POLICY
The Problem
Airlines operate under strict DOT regulations; an AI hallucinating a flight refund, voucher, or safety advice creates legal liability.
The Decision
Built a deterministic policy gate: automatically escalates cancellations, baggage loss, profanity, medical needs, and low-confidence predictions to human agents.
The Proof
40.0% of test inquiries escalated to human specialists with stated justifications; zero unauthorized compensation promises made.
Ruled Out
Permissive generative auto-replies that hallucinate booking refunds or flight guarantees.
D14 — Grounded Historical Synthesis instead of Blind Verbatim Copying
RAG SYNTHESIS
The Problem
Naive RAG systems often copy-paste old historical tweets verbatim, accidentally regurgitating other customers' names and handles.
The Decision
Indexed 1,500 verified brand resolutions in a FAISS vector store as grounding facts, synthesizing fresh, personalized responses with DM privacy protection.
The Proof
Achieved a perfect 5.0 / 5.0 Grounding Quality Score and 0.7485 Ragas Faithfulness with zero personal data leakage.
Ruled Out
Dumping raw historical tweet text verbatim, or unconstrained hallucinated LLM generation.
D15 — Empirical Human-Judge Agreement Calibration
EVALUATION HARNESS
The Problem
Automated LLM judges can be overly lenient or misaligned with actual customer expectations unless validated against human ratings.
The Decision
Benchmarked automated judge scores against independent human expert audits across Grounding, Relevance, Tone, and Actionability.
The Proof
Proved strong positive correlation (r = 0.628) and a tiny mean absolute difference of only 0.30 points on a 5-point scale.
Ruled Out
Trusting uncalibrated LLM judge metrics without human agreement proof.
